# Historical editorial policy (Stage 2; not yet published)

- Each article states: period and dates where established, context, events, people and institutions, primary evidence, scholarly interpretations, areas of disagreement, limits of the evidence, citations.
- Evidence order: archival documents, original legislation, archaeological reports (ASI), official archival catalogues (National Archives of India), peer-reviewed scholarship, reputable academic publishers. Government publications are primary sources, not the only interpretation.
- Established facts and interpretation are visibly separated. Disagreement is shown, not resolved by omission.
- India's past is not presented as one unchanged state with fixed borders; territorial and administrative change is shown on the map over time.
- No invented quotation, date, person or source: every quote goes through G4 against a stored copy of its source. Articles are human-reviewed before publication; the data auto-merge rule (D-005) does not apply to historical narrative.
